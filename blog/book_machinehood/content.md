# Book review: 'Machinehood' by S.B. Divya

**Dated**: 2026/05/24

![Book cover, Machinehood](cover.jpg)

_Why I picked it_: Saw some tweets about this book and it caught my eye - an Indian origin
author, a well received science fiction novel, and a female perspective. Before this book,
of what I have read before, it was a null set.

Overall, I quite liked the book, the world building was fascinating and quite different from
what I've seen or read before. I would definitely tag the book as cyberpunk as it has themes common
in some of the most well known works of it, but in some ways it is the opposite of that - some of the
main characters are working for (or with) the U.S. government as military operatives for example. It's
these juxtapositions that sometimes made it just a little bit hard for me to imagine how the future looks
like as described in the novel. Some more examples of this are of things that make sense right now in
present day Chennai, for example - vegetable hawkers, tea stalls, fishermen, they all exist in the world
as described in the novel, there's added high tech, but it's hard for me to completely accept them as
described.

It might be a failure of my imagination (I did not bat an eye at the Chinatown markets in near
future media I've seen), and it's definitely done in a very sensible way, but I was trying to find flaws
in it just because it is not neo-Tokyo or Hong Kong streets as shown or described in other existing
media - and this is exactly where I realized this is a really well written book, standing on its own
feet and not same-ey as I imagine a lot of science fiction with similar themes tends to be.

It did take me a long time to finish this book, so I think the pacing is not great in some parts, and
the ending kind of fizzled out. I'd have to re-read the last couple of chapters to remind myself what
exactly happened - so that's 1 star off for me.

I also really liked the writing of one of the characters, Nithya. This might be one  of the few science
fiction books that has somewhat real and relatable (even if in the near-future) adult characters
dealing with adult things - jobs, chores, abortions, relationships, family, while not being just
describable as "has daily life - but in the future". Compared to this, much of the cyberpunk media
focuses on the characters that would be in general considered outside the social norm (in the present),
and in general that makes for more interesting characters and story, but not always I guess.

Overall I really liked and recommend this book!
